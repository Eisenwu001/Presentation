The BinSnap pipeline processes reports through seven systematic stages: citizen report ingestion, automated verification, quantity estimation, spatial clustering, route planning, driver dispatch, and administrative archival. To safeguard data integrity, citizen uploads pass through a five-layer verification funnel: Layer One validates camera EXIF metadata within a twenty-four-hour capture window. Layer Two computes difference perceptual hashing to identify duplicate submissions within a ten-bit Hamming distance. Layer Three applies a synthetic image filter to flag artificial content above a zero-point-fifty confidence threshold. Layer Four deploys a MobileNetV2 waste classifier requiring a minimum confidence of zero-point-sixty. Layer Five routes borderline reports into an administrative review queue. Verified piles map to three calibrated volumetric categories: zero-point-eight, one-point-two, and one-point-six cubic meters.

Individual citizen complaints are clustered geographically using Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN). Using a spatial radius epsilon of thirty meters and a minimum points parameter of three, the algorithm groups dense report clusters along M.V. Delos Santos Street while classifying isolated reports as noise. Clusters are prioritized using our multi-factor scoring formula: Priority equals zero-point-thirty-five times normalized volume, plus zero-point-twenty-five times flood hazard factor, plus zero-point-twenty-five times unresolved age, plus zero-point-fifteen times citizen corroboration. Piles located within canal drainage buffer zones receive hazard elevation, ensuring urgent flood risks are mitigated first.

PRIORITY VOLUME
Baseline: nearest-neighbor heuristic choosing the nearest fitting stop, ignoring priority. Capacity limits drop low-priority stops with penalties. Distances and times come from OpenStreetMap via OSRM in the study; here they are straight-line schematic units.
Cleanup collection is modeled as a Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (CVRPTW). The objective minimizes total transit distance and priority-weighted penalties while respecting the fixed volumetric limit of the barangay vehicle. Google OR-Tools solves this constraint integer program. In our schematic simulation, a baseline nearest-neighbor heuristic follows geographic proximity, collecting low-priority piles until capacity is exhausted. In contrast, Google OR-Tools optimizes the global sequence, visiting high-priority canal hotspots first and achieving a fifteen-point-five percent reduction in transit distance.
